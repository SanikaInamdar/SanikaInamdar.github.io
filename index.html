Hey, I’m Sanika, a backend engineer based in Pune, working where cloud infrastructure meets applied AI.

I’m a Software Engineer at Espressif Systems, on the Cloud Backend Engineering team behind ESP RainMaker and ESP Private Agents. I design, build and own high-throughput services for an enterprise-scale cloud IoT platform — database schemas, async batch pipelines and admin control APIs, written in Go on AWS serverless — and I’m increasingly bringing agentic AI and RAG systems into that stack. I joined Espressif as an Associate Software Engineer in June 2024, and I’m an AWS Certified Developer – Associate.

My interests are distributed systems, system design and scalable backend infrastructure. In my final year at PICT, I co-built a distributed MQTT broker — a load-balanced, Redis-synchronised architecture for large-scale IoT messaging — sponsored and mentored by Golain. The work was published at IEEE ESCI 2024 and won 2nd runner-up at INC 2024 (Impetus and Concepts). On the side, I work through MIT’s distributed systems labs (MapReduce, Raft) in Go.

Before that, I interned as a Software Engineer at Mastercard in the Product & Engineering unit, improving the Regression Monitor platform of Mastercard Payment Gateway Services (MPGS), used by 42+ teams worldwide. At Azodha, I migrated a React website to Next.js for server-side rendering and built MongoDB schemas and Node.js APIs.

I graduated from Pune Institute of Computer Technology in 2024 with a B.E. in Computer Engineering and Honours in AI & ML (CGPA 9.30). I was a senior member of the PICT ACM Student Chapter (PASC), where I led the Web & App Dev event, and a Harvard WECode scholar.

Outside work, I enjoy elocution, portrait sketching, canvas painting, badminton and cricket. I also write about interview experiences on Medium.

Feel free to reach out about backend, distributed systems or cloud IoT — I’d be glad to hear from you.

publications

  1. IEEE ESCI
    Distributed MQTT Broker: A Load-Balanced Redis-Based Architecture
    Rohan Doshi, Sanika Inamdar, Tanmay Karmarkar, and others
    Abs URL

    The Internet of Things (IoT) has revolutionized data transfer in industries through the MQTT protocol, a lightweight publish-subscribe messaging protocol. Traditional MQTT brokers are single-node setups, while distributed brokers offer scalability, redundancy, load balancing and fault tolerance for large-scale IoT deployments. The proposed Load-Balancer Redis-based architecture for distributed MQTT brokers overcomes the shortcomings of traditional brokers: the Load-Balancer transfers all incoming TCP packets to the brokers for processing, ensuring equal utilization of each broker node, and Redis acts as a synchronization layer for the brokers where state information is stored.
